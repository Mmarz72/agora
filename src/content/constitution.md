## Preamble

Agora takes its name from the public square of ancient Athens, where citizens gathered to argue about the questions of the day. Agora proclaims that it has been founded and is dedicated to promote and encourage, amongst its members:

1. *The close, weekly reading of serious texts in philosophy, politics, economics, technology and artificial intelligence, history, and any other subject that bears on how we live and govern ourselves;*
2. *Discussion across academic disciplines, so that a student of computer science, a student of classics, and a student of economics may test their ideas against one another on a common text;*
3. *Disagreement conducted in good faith, in which members argue against the strongest version of a view rather than the weakest, and in which no idea is shielded from examination;*
4. *The habit of connecting enduring texts to current events, so that members read Thucydides alongside the morning news and a new economics paper alongside Adam Smith;*
5. *An open forum, free of charge and without prerequisite, where any Princeton undergraduate may take part regardless of background, major, or prior reading.*

## Article I: Name

**Section 1.** The name of this student organization shall be *AGORA*.

**Section 2.** The word “Agora,” together with the organization’s logo, shall not be used in connection with Princeton University by any individual or group of individuals without the consent of the officers or the organization as a whole.

## Article II: Membership

**Section 1.** Membership in this organization will be extended to all undergraduate students at Princeton University who demonstrate interest in membership. Interest is demonstrated by joining the organization’s mailing list or attending a weekly session.

**Section 2.** No person who has expressed interest in membership shall be denied membership. Agora is open to all Princeton University students regardless of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, ancestry, age, marital or domestic partnership status, veteran status, disability, or any other characteristic protected by law or University policy.

**Section 3.** There shall be no membership dues, and no application or selection process for membership. No member shall be required to purchase any book or text; the organization shall provide all assigned readings free of charge.

**Section 4.** Any member may attend sessions, propose readings, propose amendments to this constitution under Article IX, and apply for a position on the Executive Board under Article IV.

**Section 5.** Graduate students, faculty, and guests may attend sessions at the invitation of the Executive Board, but may not hold office.

## Article III: Officers

**Section 1.** There shall be an Executive Board of Officers, which shall consist of the following: two Co-Presidents, a Vice President, a Treasurer, a Secretary, and a Director of Readings. No person shall hold more than one office at the same time.

**Section 2.** The Executive Board shall be empowered to:

1. Conduct the business and affairs of the organization during periods between meetings of the members;
2. Act as arbiters in cases of disagreement among members;
3. Represent the organization in cases of liability, responsibility or honors;
4. Approve the reading schedule for each semester, as set out in Article V;
5. Select the members of the Executive Board and the Co-Presidents, as set out in Article IV;
6. Amend this constitution, as set out in Article IX.

**Section 3.** The duties of the officers shall be as follows:

1. The Co-Presidents shall jointly serve as the chief officers of the organization, preside over meetings of the Executive Board, act as the organization’s representatives to the University, the Undergraduate Student Government, and the Office of the Dean of Undergraduate Students, and oversee the conduct of weekly sessions. Either Co-President may act alone on routine matters. Where the Co-Presidents disagree on a matter that requires a decision, the matter shall be decided by a majority vote of the Executive Board.
2. The Vice President shall assist the Co-Presidents, assume their duties in the absence of both, coordinate outreach and recruitment of new members, and serve as the organization’s liaison to faculty, academic departments, and guest speakers.
3. The Treasurer shall manage all funds of the organization, prepare a budget each semester, submit funding requests to the Undergraduate Student Government and other University sources, keep accurate records of all income and expenditures, and ensure that all spending complies with University financial policy.
4. The Secretary shall maintain the organization’s mailing list and attendance records, record the minutes of Executive Board meetings and selection decisions, distribute readings and session announcements to members, and keep the official copy of this constitution.
5. The Director of Readings shall solicit reading proposals from members, prepare the reading schedule each semester, appoint a Discussion Leader for each session as set out in Article V, secure readings in a form that can be freely distributed in compliance with copyright law and University policy, and ensure that discussion questions are circulated before each session.

**Section 4.** Meetings of the Executive Board shall be held whenever necessary, upon the call of either Co-President or at the request of at least three officers. A majority of the Executive Board shall constitute a quorum, and unless this constitution provides otherwise, the Executive Board shall act by a majority vote of the officers present.

## Article IV: Selection of the Executive Board

**Section 1.** Application process:

1. Members of the Executive Board shall be selected each year through an application process held at the start of the Spring semester, for a term of one year or until the successor for each respective position has been duly selected. Officers may reapply for any position.
2. The Executive Board shall announce the application process to all members at least two weeks before applications are due, listing each open position and its duties.
3. Any member of the organization may apply. Each applicant shall submit a written application stating the position or positions sought, the applicant’s involvement in Agora, and the applicant’s plans for the organization. The Executive Board may also interview applicants.
4. The Executive Board shall evaluate applicants on their commitment to the organization, their record of participation, and the quality of their plans. No applicant shall be evaluated on any basis prohibited by Article II, Section 2.
5. The outgoing Executive Board shall select the incoming officers by majority vote. An officer who has applied for a position shall not vote on the selection for that position.

**Section 2.** Selection of the Co-Presidents:

1. Co-President shall be an Executive Board position for which members apply under Section 1 in the same manner as any other position. The outgoing Executive Board shall select the two Co-Presidents from among the applicants for that position by majority vote, subject to Section 3 of this Article.
2. The Executive Board may select a single member to serve as sole President when only one suitable candidate is available. A sole President shall hold all powers this constitution grants to the Co-Presidents.

**Section 3.** Founding Co-Presidents:

1. The founders of Agora, Holden Demain and Ammar Omar, shall serve as the Founding Co-Presidents.
2. Each Founding Co-President shall remain in office, without standing for selection under Section 1 or Section 2, for as long as that person remains an undergraduate student at Princeton University and chooses to serve, unless removed under Article VII.
3. A Founding Co-President may relinquish office at any time by written notice to the Executive Board. The Executive Board shall then select a successor Co-President under Section 2, and the successor shall serve a regular one-year term subject to the application process in Section 1.
4. While at least one Founding Co-President remains in office, the selection of any other Co-President shall require that Founding Co-President’s approval.

## Article V: Meetings and Readings

**Section 1.** Agora shall meet weekly on Sunday afternoons during the academic year, excluding University breaks, reading periods, and examination periods. The Executive Board may reschedule an individual session when necessary.

**Section 2.** Each session shall be organized around a common reading. Readings shall be chosen to be completed in roughly ninety minutes and shall be distributed to members no later than the Wednesday preceding the session.

**Section 3.** Any member may propose a reading to the Director of Readings. The Director of Readings shall present a draft schedule of dates and topics to the Executive Board before the start of each semester, and the Executive Board shall approve it by majority vote. The schedule shall include readings drawn from philosophy, politics, economics, and technology over the course of each semester, and shall reserve sessions for current developments that arise during the term.

**Section 4.** For each session, the Director of Readings may appoint a Discussion Leader, who may be any member of the organization. The Discussion Leader shall select or confirm the reading for that session in consultation with the Director of Readings, prepare and circulate discussion questions, introduce the reading, and moderate the discussion. Where no Discussion Leader is appointed, the Director of Readings or an officer designated by the Co-Presidents shall lead the session. The Director of Readings shall seek to give as many members as possible the opportunity to lead a session.

**Section 5.** Sessions shall be conducted under the following norms: members shall address arguments rather than persons; members shall state the strongest version of a view before criticizing it; and remarks made in sessions shall not be attributed to individual members outside the session without their consent.

**Section 6.** Agora takes no institutional position on any political, religious, or ideological question and shall not endorse any candidate for public or student office. Its purpose is to host the argument, not to settle it.

**Section 7.** The Executive Board may invite faculty members, graduate students, or outside speakers to introduce a reading or join a session.

## Article VI: Finances

**Section 1.** The organization shall charge no dues. Its funds shall come from the Undergraduate Student Government, the Office of the Dean of Undergraduate Students, academic departments and programs, and other sources permitted by University policy.

**Section 2.** Funds shall be used for the reproduction of readings, refreshments at sessions, honoraria and hospitality for invited speakers, and other expenses that advance the purposes stated in the Preamble.

**Section 3.** Any single expenditure exceeding two hundred dollars ($200) shall require the approval of both the Treasurer and a Co-President. The Treasurer shall present a financial report to the Executive Board at the end of each semester.

**Section 4.** All financial activity shall be conducted through the organization’s University account and in accordance with University policy. No officer shall hold organization funds in a personal account.

## Article VII: Removal of Officers and Vacancies

**Section 1.** An officer who neglects the duties of the office or acts contrary to this constitution or to University policy may be removed by a two-thirds vote of the other members of the Executive Board, at a meeting of which all officers have received at least one week’s notice. The officer in question shall be given the opportunity to respond before the vote.

**Section 2.** If an office becomes vacant before the end of its term, the Executive Board shall appoint a member of the organization to serve until the next regular selection under Article IV. If a Co-Presidency becomes vacant, the Executive Board shall fill it under Article IV, Section 2, and the remaining Co-President shall serve as sole President until the vacancy is filled.

## Article VIII: Miscellaneous Provisions

**Section 1.** Agora and its members shall abide by Rights, Rules, Responsibilities and all other policies of Princeton University, and by the policies of the Undergraduate Student Government and the Office of the Dean of Undergraduate Students. Where this constitution conflicts with University policy, University policy shall govern.

**Section 2.** Hazing in any form is prohibited. Participation in Agora shall never be conditioned on any act other than attendance and good-faith discussion.

**Section 3.** The organization shall maintain a public record of its reading schedule so that any Princeton undergraduate may read along and attend without prior notice.

**Section 4.** Any website or public materials of the organization shall state that Agora is open to all Princeton University students.

## Article IX: Amendments

**Section 1.** This constitution may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the entire Executive Board, provided that the text of the proposed amendment has been circulated to all officers at least one week before the vote. While at least one Founding Co-President remains in office, any amendment to Article IV, Section 3 or to this Article shall also require the approval of each Founding Co-President then in office.

**Section 2.** Any member of the organization may submit a proposed amendment in writing to the Executive Board, which shall consider it at its next meeting.

**Section 3.** The Secretary shall notify all members of any adopted amendment within one week of its adoption, and the Co-Presidents shall submit the amended constitution to the Undergraduate Student Government and the Office of the Dean of Undergraduate Students as University policy requires.

## Article X: Ratification

**Section 1.** This constitution will take effect following a unanimous vote of approval by the founding Executive Board.

**Section 2.** The founding officers other than the Founding Co-Presidents shall serve from ratification until the first regular selection under Article IV, which shall take place no later than the start of the Spring semester following the organization’s first full semester of recognition.
